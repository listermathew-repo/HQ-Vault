**You:** how much was 81gbp 17 years ago in AUD

**Claude:** Seventeen years ago from now puts us around **March 2009**. Around March 26, 2009, the GBP/AUD exchange rate was approximately 2.11.

So **£81 GBP would have been worth roughly A$171 AUD** at that time (81 × 2.11 = ~170.91).

Worth noting that 2009 was a volatile year for this pair — the rate ranged from about 1.74 in October up to about 2.26 in February, so the exact amount would depend on which month the conversion happened. In early 2009 you'd have gotten more AUD (~A$180), while by late 2009 it would have been less (~A$145).

**You:** A$171 AUD *75

**Claude:** A$171 × 75 = **A$12,825**

**You:** what is the equivalent today taking into consdering cpi etc

**Claude:** Based on Australian CPI data, $100 in 2009 is equivalent to approximately $153.75 in 2026, reflecting cumulative inflation of about 53.75% over that period (averaging around 2.56% per year).

So applying that to your figure:

**A$12,825 in 2009 ≈ A$19,720 in today's dollars** (12,825 × 1.5375).

**You:** A$171 AUD *40

**Claude:** A$171 × 40 = **A$6,840**

**You:** and the equivalent today?

**Claude:** Using the current GBP/AUD rate of approximately 1.92, £81 × 40 = **£3,240 GBP** would be worth about **A$6,221 AUD** today.

So you'd actually get around **A$619 less** now than you would have in March 2009, because the pound has weakened against the Aussie dollar since then (2.11 → 1.92).

**You:** what is the equivelent of A$6,840 17 years ago to today?

**Claude:** Based on Australian CPI data, A$100 in 2009 is equivalent to about A$153.75 in 2026, reflecting an average inflation rate of around 2.56% per year.

So **A$6,840 in 2009 would be equivalent to roughly A$10,517 today** in terms of purchasing power (6,840 × 1.5375).

**You:** if I earn this a week what would be my annual salary

**Claude:** A$10,517 × 52 weeks = **A$546,884 per year**.
