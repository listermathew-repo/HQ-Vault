## The Professional Employees Award — this is the substantial new ground

You've uploaded MA000065. Note that "Professional Employees Award 2020" (what Capgemini ticked on the F3) and "Professional Employees Award 2010" are the same instrument under the same MA code — the award was updated and renamed during the 4-yearly review. Consultation obligations carry through.

**Clause 9 — Consultation about major workplace change** — applies when the employer "makes a definite decision to make major changes in ... organisation [or] structure ... likely to have significant effects on employees." Clause 9.5(a) expressly lists "termination of employment" as a significant effect. A redundancy is squarely covered.

The obligations are:

1. **9.1(a)** — give notice of the changes to all affected employees.
2. **9.1(b)** — discuss with them *and their representatives* (i) the introduction of the changes, (ii) their likely effect, and (iii) *measures to avoid or reduce adverse effects*.
3. **9.1(c)** — commence discussions *as soon as practicable after a definite decision has been made*.
4. **9.2** — give in writing *all relevant information* about the changes including nature, expected effect on employees, and any other matters likely to affect employees.
5. **9.4** — *promptly consider* any matters raised by the employees.

Against what the F3 describes (paragraphs 3.4–3.5) and what the case file records of the 26 February meeting:

| Obligation | What clause 9 requires | What happened |
|---|---|---|
| Written information in advance | "give in writing ... all relevant information" — nature, expected effect, other matters | Letter sent *after* the meeting (paragraph 3.5 says "Later on 26 February 2026, Capgemini sent to Mr Lister ... a letter confirming..."). One day's notice of the meeting itself with no agenda or supporting documents |
| Discussion of measures to avoid/mitigate | Explicit requirement under 9.1(b)(iii) | 11-minute meeting with pre-written letter; no documented discussion of alternatives |
| Prompt consideration of matters raised | 9.4 | Two requests for legal advice ignored; support person's effective participation denied |
| Discussion with representative | 9.1(b) refers to representatives | Support person restricted to emotional support only; microphone dispute |

**Why this matters legally.** Section 389(1)(b) of the FW Act provides that for a dismissal to be a "genuine redundancy" the employer must have complied with any obligation in a modern award that applied to the employment to consult about the redundancy. If clause 9 applied (and Capgemini's own F3 ticks that it did), and Capgemini cannot demonstrate written information in advance, a genuine discussion of measures to avert or mitigate, and prompt consideration of matters raised, then the dismissal fails the genuine redundancy defence at s.389(1)(b).

This matters strategically because Capgemini's *entire* jurisdictional objection rests on s.389. Paragraph 2.2 of the F3 objects only on genuine redundancy grounds. If s.389(1)(b) fails, the jurisdictional objection fails, and the Commission moves to a substantive s.387 assessment where the procedural points (11 minutes, no agenda, no documents, support person denied effective participation, two ignored requests for legal advice) are already strong.

Practically: the F3 itself gives you the foundation. Paragraph 3.5 admits the consultation letter was sent "later on 26 February" — i.e., after the meeting. Clause 9.2 requires written information for the purposes of the discussion, which means before or at least during, not after.
