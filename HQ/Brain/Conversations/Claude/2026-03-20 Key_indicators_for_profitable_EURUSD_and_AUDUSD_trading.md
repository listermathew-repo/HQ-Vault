The Rubber Band Concept: The principle that after a strong impulsive move, price is drawn back to the "base" or "ceiling" where the move began, seeking to mitigate all price areas between discount and premium levels

Wyckoff Phases: The market cycles through four phases: Accumulation, Markup, Market Bias and Structure (The Foundation)

Key Price Levels and Zones (Where to Look)
* Areas of Accumulation / Distribution: Consolidations where major orders were filled prior to a strong "leg up" or "leg down". TJR actively waits for price to draw back into these zones.
* Order Blocks (Building Blocks): Specific areas (sometimes playfully referred to by TJR as an "order cock") that caused a previous expansion in price. He marks high-timeframe order blocks to find overall bias, and lower-timeframe order blocks for precise entries.
* Fair Value Gaps (FVG) and Imbalances: Gaps left in price action after a violent move. Filling an FVG is frequently used as an entry confirmation.
* Equilibrium: The 50% midpoint of a Fair Value Gap or an order block, which serves as a highly reactive price point.
* Floors and Ceilings: While TJR predominantly uses SMC rather than classic retail support/resistance, he does occasionally note when a "ceiling turns into a floor" during a breakout retest.
Liquidity and Execution Triggers (When to Enter)
* Liquidity Sweeps: A mandatory step in his Forex strategy where price temporarily dips below or pushes above a prominent high/low to trap traders before reversing.
* Session Highs and Lows: He specifically looks to sweep the liquidity resting at the previous session's extremes, heavily targeting London session lows/highs, New York session lows/highs, and Asian session highs.
* Third Confluence (Retest & Reaction): To avoid fake-outs, TJR often waits for extra confirmation: a break of structure, followed by a pullback into an order block or FVG, and finally a definitive reaction candle (such as a 1-minute or 5-minute bullish/bearish candle closure) before executing his entry.
* Wyckoff's Method (Spring): He occasionally references Wyckoff logic—identifying accumulation, watching for the "spring" (liquidity sweep) out of the range, and entering on the breakout.
Exit Indicators (Take Profits and Stop Losses)
* High-Confluence Areas (Take Profits): Exits are not set at random ratios or using Fibonacci levels; they are placed at logical zones where price might naturally react and reverse. This includes un-swept prominent highs/lows, higher timeframe order blocks, and previous accumulation areas.
* Base vs. Wick of Candles: When setting take profits based on previous highs/lows, he specifically targets the area from the "base of the candle down to the wick".
* Invalidation Points (Stop Losses): Stop losses are placed strictly underneath (or above) the absolute low/high of the recent liquidity sweep or accumulation area. If price crosses this point, the trade setup is structurally invalidated.
