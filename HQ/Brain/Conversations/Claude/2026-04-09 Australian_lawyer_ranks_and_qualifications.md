**Claude:** In Australia, lawyers don't have formal "ranks" in the way some professions do, but there are important distinctions and ways to assess experience and expertise:

## Key Distinctions

**Admission Level**
- **Solicitors** and **barristers** are the two main legal professions (though this distinction is blurring with "legal practitioners")
- Both must be admitted to practice by their state or territory law society/bar association

**Seniority Markers (not official ranks)**
- **Years of practice** – publicly listed on bar association websites and legal directories
- **Specialization** – lawyers often specialize in areas like commercial law, family law, criminal defense, etc.
- **Silk/QC status** – A barrister or senior lawyer can be appointed as a "Queen's Counsel" (QC) or "Senior Counsel" (SC) depending on the state. This is a prestigious honor recognizing senior practitioners with significant expertise. It's not automatic – lawyers apply and are assessed.

## How to Check Credentials

1. **Legal Directories**
   - Law Society websites (NSW Law Society, Victorian Bar, etc.)
   - Search for accreditation, years admitted, complaints history
   - Many list specializations

2. **Bar Association Websites**
   - Lists barristers with their qualifications and practice areas
   - Shows if they hold QC/SC status

3. **Online Legal Directories**
   - Websites like *Lawvine*, *TrustLaw*, or law firm websites often list:
     - Education (university, degrees)
     - Years of experience
     - Specializations
     - Client testimonials or case outcomes

4. **Ask Direct Questions**
   - Experience in your specific legal area
   - Track record with similar cases
   - Whether they have relevant accreditations (e.g., family law specialist accreditation)

## Red Flags vs. Green Flags

**Green flags:** QC/SC status, specialist accreditation, detailed case experience in your area, professional references

**Red flags:** Unwilling to discuss experience, disciplinary history, vague about qualifications

Since you're in Adelaide, you'd typically look at the **Law Society of South Australia** website for credentials and the **South Australian Bar Association** for barristers.
