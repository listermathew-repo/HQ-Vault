**Gemini:** The file **JMP\_TheLongGame\_Module1\_Why This Works - The Power of Compounding.docx** is the first module of a course called "The Long Game: A Simple Investing Framework for Australians," provided by JMP Strategic Investments.  
  
The module's focus is on explaining **compounding**, which it describes as the single most powerful force in investing.  
  
Here are the key takeaways from the document:The Power of Compounding

  - **What it is:** Compounding is the process where an investment generates returns on both the original amount invested and all previously earned returns. Over long periods, this process accelerates growth significantly.
  - **Time is the most important ingredient:** Compounding requires only a return on your money and **time**. Starting early is described as the single most important decision an investor can make.
  - **The Cost of Waiting:** The document illustrates that delaying investing by just 10 years (from age 25 to 35) can result in a portfolio that is approximately **$1,340,000 less** at retirement, despite only contributing $48,000 less in total. This demonstrates that every year of delay costs you exponentially.

Why Market Timing Destroys Compounding

  - **Missing the Best Days:** The most dangerous thing an investor can do is try to move in and out of the market to avoid bad periods. The best-performing days in market history occur overwhelmingly during **bear markets and periods of crisis**.
  - **The Cost:** Missing just 10 of the best trading days over a 20-year period can cut your final investment outcome by more than half compared to an investor who stayed fully invested. The strategy is to be invested when the markets recover, even if it feels frightening.

Understanding Returns and Inflation

  - **Nominal vs. Real Returns:** The document distinguishes between **Nominal Return** (the raw percentage return before adjusting for inflation) and **Real Return** (the return after subtracting inflation, which reflects true increase in purchasing power).
  - **Historical Averages:** Based on long-run global equity data, the average nominal return is approximately **10% per annum**, and the real return is approximately **7% per annum**. The compounding argument remains powerful even when using the inflation-adjusted 7% real return figure.

Compounding as a Framework

  - **Automation:** The document suggests using compounding as a decision-making framework. The most important habit is **automation**—setting up an automatic investment contribution on the day income arrives to "Pay yourself first" before the money is available to spend.
  - **Australian Context:** For Australian investors, two factors make compounding particularly powerful: the **Superannuation** system (an "invisible compounding engine") and **Franking Credits** (which increase the after-tax return on Australian share investments).

The overall message is that compounding is a "force of nature applied to mathematics," and the investor's job is simply to start early, invest consistently, stay invested through downturns, and let time work.
