**Gemini:** Here is a study plan for the trading concepts discussed in the video:Study Plan for Trading Concepts  
  
**Step 1: Determine Market Direction and Leg**

  - Identify the market direction (sell side or buy side).
  - Determine the current leg the market is in on the higher time frame, specifically the hourly.
  - The current example in the video focuses on the sell side of the curve, meaning the primary focus is on identifying shorts.

**Step 2: Utilize a Fibonacci Retracement**

  - Employ a Fibonacci retracement tool to visualize the overall leg.
  - Identify the premium and discount areas.
  - Understand that anything above 50% is considered premium in a down leg.
  - Note the market tends to react off the 50%, 61.8%, and 79% retracement levels.
  - A close above the annotated box (green box) would be needed for a change of trend from sell side to buy side.

**Step 3: Implement Trading Tools and Strategies**  
  
Once the market side is determined, and a time slot for trading is selected, the following tools and strategies can be utilized:

1.  **Exponential Moving Averages (EMAs):**
      - This tool is primarily for a highly trending market.
      - The strategy involves looking for a reaction when the market pulls back, for example, the 10 EMA over the 20 EMA when trending to the downside. (Note: The speaker calls this their "least favorite" method because markets do not often trend highly).
2.  **Drawing Levels (Ceilings and Floors):**
      - Drawing levels on the 15-minute chart is paramount.
      - The market tends to respect these levels, providing points of interest.
      - Look for a deeper retracement into the annotated level, especially a 50% range between the high and the low, as this tends to have the best reaction.
      - Identify ceilings created on the 15-minute chart for shorting opportunities.
3.  **Market Structure Break (Break of Structure):**
      - Look for a structural break of the market's movement.
      - The ideal trade involves the market breaking structure, coming back in, and providing a trade to the downside.
      - The market may come back into a 61.8% retracement, break structure, and then come back into an imbalance or a little ceiling before selling off.
4.  **Volume Weighted Average Price (VWAP):**
      - Use the VWAP for a mean reversion trade back to the volume-weighted average price (the blue line in the middle).
      - Ideally, the market breaks a prior session or prior day high or low, and the trade goes in the direction of the trend.
      - VWAP can serve as a target for scaling down, particularly when the market is extended.
      - The VWAP can also be used to observe choppy markets, where the market trades beneath and above the VWAP, hitting the extremes.
      - **VWAP Settings:** The speaker's preference is to use the two extremes (number two and number three) for the standard deviation and to remove the filled colors for the bands.

**Step 4: Review and Refinement**

  - Allocate a specific time slot for trading.
  - Practice applying the strategies discussed: trading off a 50% or 61% range retracement, using EMAs for trending markets, market structure breaks off a level, and implementing the VWAP.
  - Select the strategy that is the most comfortable and "cleanly" visible to you.
